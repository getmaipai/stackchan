## 2. The pipeline and the rule behind it

Cheapest first, no sound direction anywhere (owner correction 2026-11-16, and the memory review: a face detector does not fit
the core image as designed).

1. Address (the wake word): look at the current view for 300 ms; if something moves there, turn to the middle of it and hold eye
   contact. If nothing moves, one search over five pan stops (-60, -30, 0, +30, +60 degrees, a stop that is within 10 degrees of
   the first look is skipped, the sweep starts at the end nearer the head), about 300 ms of camera at each, stop at the first
   mover, give up and return to center.
2. Notice (setting "always" only): one frame a second at rest; somebody walks in, turn once, say nothing, return to center 20 s
   after the last sighting. Never a search, never wandering.
3. Follow: while a talk goes on, a moving person is followed in small slow corrections (at least 8 degrees off, at most one a
   2 s; a step of 15 degrees or more spends one of the four turns).
4. A face detector, in a separate build only, decides "who" at the pan stop: the blob says where, the face confirms (`ms_init` with
   `detector` set, tested with a stand-in detector).

Limits that never bend, all tested: 4 turns a minute (a search and its turn count once, the return to center is free); never a
turn for a target seen more than 3 s ago; camera bursts at least 1 s apart, each with the camera-on cue request; slower moves
(55 percent speed) in quiet mode and at night; no camera and no head move while muted, in privacy sleep, with vision off, in
full quiet, asleep, with the setting off, or while a host holds the lease; a refused camera (memory admission) keeps the seeker
away for 10 s; a camera that delivers nothing ends a search instead of hanging it.

## 3. The core (S10)

- Frame difference on a 40 by 30 gray picture. A change of the mean brightness over 22 levels is a light change and refused;
  smaller shifts are subtracted first (tested with a 12 level shift and a person in it). A pixel counts at 20 levels; a pixel
  survives only with two changed neighbors (isolated speckle vanishes); the largest 4-connected blob of at least 10 pixels wins,
  a tie goes to the one nearer the middle; more than 55 percent of the picture changed is the camera or the light, not a person.
  Angle from the pixel column: 65 degrees across 40 pixels (`MS_FOV_DEG`); the sign of the picture axis is one constant
  (`MS_IMAGE_X_SIGN`) to check on the unit.
- Memory: `sizeof(ms_t)` is 4,996 bytes (one previous frame and the labeling scratch). Allocate it in PSRAM. No heap use, no
  ESP call, no clock of its own.
- Tests (`host/tests/firmware/seek_core_test.c`, wrapped by `host/tests/test_firmware_seek_core.py`) use a simulated head and
  synthetic frames: a mover in view, a mover only visible from the -60 stop, a sweep that finds nobody (five camera bursts,
  stops in the stated order, back at center), a still person (honest limit: motion cannot see a person who never moves, only the
  face detector build closes that), light flicker (a 40 level flicker and a 14 level one), two movers (the bigger wins), the notice
  and the 20 s return, follow, 6 wake words in a minute (the fifth and sixth do nothing, the one after a minute works), a stale
  target (held 3.5 s, no turn; held 1 s, one turn), every gate, a refused camera, a camera that delivers nothing, and the
  lease against the seeker.

## 4. The owner lease

`self.look.claim {owner, ttl_s}`, `self.look.release {owner}`, `self.look.status` (the tools are S12; the logic is in S10).
Owner names are lowercase letters, digits, dash, dot, underscore, up to 23. The time to live is clamped to 5 to 60 s, 0 means 15 s.
A second claimant is refused while the first holds; only the holder releases; the same owner claiming again renews; expiry is
exact and survives the 32-bit clock wrap; a reboot keeps nothing (the host claims again at the next connect). Status says
`owner`: `unit` (this robot seeks), `host` (a lease is held) or `none` (setting off), plus the holder, the time left, the last face,
the setting and the counters.

## 5. The setting and the host side

- Unit setting `turn_to_faces`: off, talk, always. The flash key is the 13 characters of its own name (the limit is 15).
  **Default is `talk`**, not `always`: the camera then runs only after the wake word. `always` keeps the camera-on cue lit almost
  all the time (one burst a second, each holding the cue for 1 s). The brief wanted `always` as the default after the memory
  gate; that stays a decision for the gate measurement, one constant in `unit_prefs.py` and one table row in S11.
- Host setting `face_owner` per unit: `robot` (default), `hub`, `off`. Page: Behavior, card "Turning toward people", with the
  "coming with the next unit update" note until the unit answers `self.look.status`.
- The Home adapter claims the lease only when: the setting says `hub`, a Home hub is configured (brain `home`, or a hub URL set;
  the local `llm` brain with no hub never claims), the unit has the look tool, and the hub's tracking really runs (camera feed
  ready, one of the two look choices on, watching not off). Anything else releases a lease it holds. Heartbeat every 5 s, ttl 15 s,
  also released at shutdown. `look_owner.py` holds all of it.
- The host's old face search (look toward the speaker, look for faces) is the hub-owned path now: it moves the head when the hub
  owns seeking, and also when the robot owns it but this unit cannot seek yet (no look tool), so nothing regresses before the
  unit updates. It never moves the head for a unit that seeks by itself, and never when the owner is `off`.
- `off` sends the unit `turn_to_faces: off` whatever the person chose; the choice is kept and sent again when the owner goes back
  to `robot`.
- Tests: `host/tests/test_look_owner.py` (17: claim, ttl, renew every 5 s, release when tracking stops or the setting changes, no
  claim without a hub, unsupported and unreachable units, a busy lease, reconnect, the search rules, the values sent) and two page
  tests in `test_settings_ui.py` (save, validate, read back, words in all six languages).

## 6. Detector evaluation

Method. Models are the official ones from the esp-dl repository (`models/human_face_detect`, `models/pedestrian_detect`, the
ESP32-S3 variants), downloaded to `~/stackchan-models/detectors/esp-dl` (nothing vendored). They are `.espdl` files that only the
esp-dl runtime on a chip can run, so a small int8 executor was written on the PC (`tools/espdl_run.py`): it reads the files with
the FlatBuffers schema that esp-ppq ships, unpacks the S3 filter layout (16 output channels per block, per the esp-ppq exporter),
runs Conv, PRelu, Add, Concat, MaxPool, Resize, Split, Sigmoid, Mul, GlobalAveragePool and RequantizeLinear in int8 with the
files' own exponents, and decodes the outputs the way the esp-dl post-processors do. Check that it is right: a wrong layout
gives flat scores, and the shipped models give boxes that sit on the test face (face box and truth within a few pixels).
Not bit-exact with the chip (rounding mode and the lookup-table sigmoid are approximated), so read rates to within a few points.

Test data. One face: the astronaut photo bundled with scikit-image (NASA, public domain). It is scaled to the width a 15 cm face
has at 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3 and 4 m on a 320 by 240 frame with a 65 degree lens (42, 28, 21, 17, 14, 11 px), pasted with a soft mask
on bundled backgrounds that contain no face, rotated up to 10 degrees, flipped at random, then lens blur and sensor noise. Light:
normal, backlit (face at 45 percent on a bright window), dim (30 percent and strong noise), bright (clipped). 40 frames per
distance and light, 960 frames, plus 320 faceless frames for false positives. No picture of a real person from the web. One face
tests range and light, not the population; accuracy across faces is the published mAP below. Everything here is a PC result:
what it says about the S3 is the model's published speed and the memory read from its graph.

Hit rate at default thresholds, normal and backlit light together (80 frames per cell), percent:

| Distance (face width) | MSR+MNP, 160x120 crop | ESPDet-Pico 224, whole frame | ESPDet-Pico 416, whole frame | ESPDet-Pico 224, native 160x120 crop | YuNet float (reference) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 m (42 px) | 17 | 92 | 91 | 98 | 100 |
| 1.5 m (28 px) | 1 | 52 | 78 | 88 | 86 |
| 2.0 m (21 px) | 0 | 33 | 61 | 70 | 48 |
| 2.5 m (17 px) | 1 | 7 | 37 | 40 | 46 |
| 3.0 m (14 px) | 0 | 5 | 21 | 21 | 26 |
| 4.0 m (11 px) | 0 | 0 | 1 | 7 | 3 |

MSR+MNP on the whole frame sampled down to 160x120 found 0 of 960 in every cell. Dim light (30 percent, noisy) cuts every
detector hard: Pico 224 whole frame 55 percent at 1 m and 15 percent at 1.5 m, then near zero; the clipped bright case is worse.
Backlit with the face underexposed to 45 percent was about as good as normal light. The 416 model in dim light fell below the
224 model, which is noise in this test more than a finding; do not read it as a rule.

False positives on 320 faceless frames: MSR+MNP 0, Pico 224 whole frame 0, Pico 416 0, Pico 224 crop 1, YuNet 1.

The native crop is the one that matters for a seeker: after the pan to a blob the person is near the middle, so a 160 by 120
window at sensor resolution, letterboxed into the 224 input, shows the face 1.4 times larger than the whole frame does. A VGA
sensor read (640 by 480) cropped to 320 by 240 doubles the face width again: a face at 3 m then looks like one at 1.5 m. That
costs a 614 KB frame buffer in PSRAM and a mode switch of the camera, and has not been tried.

Published numbers (esp-dl README, ESP32-S3 at 240 MHz, PSRAM at 80 MHz, int8) and sizes read from the files:

| Model | Input | Time on the S3 (pre, model, post) | mAP50-95 (their set) | File (flash) | Peak live activations (PSRAM) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MSR s8 v1 | 120x160 | 3.7 + 32.5 + 0.3 ms | 0.367 for MSR+MNP | 61 KB | 141 KB |
| MNP s8 v1 (per candidate) | 48x48 | 0.6 + 5.9 + 0.1 ms | | 130 KB | 50 KB |
| ESPDet-Pico face 224 | 224x224 | 7.3 + 129.4 + 0.7 ms (137 ms) | 0.504 | 480 KB | 343 KB |
| ESPDet-Pico face 416 | 416x416 | 21.8 + 422.1 + 1.1 ms (445 ms) | 0.598 | 499 KB | 1,183 KB |
| Pedestrian Pico | 224x224 | 10.6 + 113.3 + 1.1 ms (125 ms) | not published | 435 KB | 392 KB |

"Peak live activations" is the sum of the int8 tensors alive at once, found from the graph: a lower bound for what the esp-dl
planner uses. Weights are read from flash. How much internal RAM a run needs (kernel scratch, descriptors, the camera's own
buffers) is not in the files and was not measured: that needs the unit.

Person detector (stretch). Same executor, 30 frames per person height, one public-domain upper-body photo (the model is trained
on whole-body pedestrians; no whole-body or seated picture was available, so this is a sanity and range check, not a rate).
At score 0.5 a person of 60 px height in the 240 px frame (chest up at about 3 m) was found in 17 of 30, 90 px in 25 of 30,
120 px and up in 26 to 30 of 30; faceless frames gave 2 of 60 detections at 0.5 and 44 of 60 at 0.25, so the default 0.5 is the
only usable threshold.

## 7. What fits where

- Core image: the motion blob and the pan-stop search (S10, S11 and the later wiring). About 5 KB in PSRAM, no model, one burst
  of the camera at a time. This is the "look for you" that ships first.
- Experiment build only (the `eyes` build or the `home` preset, behind `MAIPAI_FACE_TRACK`, with the admission control from the
  memory review: 40 KB free internal, 24 KB largest block, camera started only then, detector run only at pan stops): the
  ESPDet-Pico 224 face model. It is the only detector that works at the room's distances: about 90 percent at 1 m, 88 percent
  at 1.5 m and 70 percent at 2 m on the native crop in normal light, 137 ms a stop (five stops are about 0.7 s of detector time),
  340 KB of weights and about 350 KB of PSRAM. It needs a place for a 0.5 MB file: the 6.2 MB assets partition has to give it up
  or the model arrives through the pack loader. Reliable range is 1.5 m, about 2 m with the crop, 3 m only with the VGA crop idea.
- Not used: MSR+MNP. It found almost nothing at QVGA at any distance (0 to 17 percent) and its second stage, as written in the
  esp-dl post-processor source, accepts a candidate when its larger class score has a probability above 0.5, which is nearly always
  true, so it refines boxes and may not reject (read from the source, not confirmed on a chip).
- Not used: ESPDet-Pico 416. Better mAP, but 445 ms a frame and 1.2 MB of activations for a few points of range.
- Person detector: stretch behind its own switch, experiment build only. 125 ms and 392 KB, finds upper bodies to about 2.5 to 3 m,
  but it is for a body the camera may not see (seated, behind a desk), and one photo is not a rate. Measure on the unit with
  real clips of the household before relying on it.

## 8. What needs the unit (patch S12 and the measurements)

Wiring, not yet written, in this order:
1. A task of the lowest priority (below audio and the servo) that calls `ms_tick` about every 50 ms with the gates the unit
   already has (mute, privacy sleep, vision off, full quiet, quiet and night, awake) and the wake word edge.
2. Camera: take the frame the existing camera path produces, shrink it to 40 by 30 gray by box averaging, feed it, drop it. Call
   `monitor_cue_begin(out.cue_ms)` on every `out.camera`. No new buffer in internal RAM.
3. Head: `out.move` goes through the existing head-move cap and gate (patch 038 and the host head gate); `head_settled` comes
   from the servo; the unit's own moves are told to the pickup detector as before.
4. Tools `self.look.claim`, `self.look.release`, `self.look.status`, a reader of the `turn_to_faces` row, registered where the
   other tools are; the lease lives in RAM only.
5. Measure on the unit: the sign of the picture axis (`MS_IMAGE_X_SIGN`) and the real field of view; the 300 ms burst cost;
   false turns per hour in a lived-in room (the idle watch at one frame a second is the risk); the memory gate with the seeker
   running through a spoken turn; `int_big` and internal free before and after; power; whether the head's own motion blurs the
   first frame after a settle (the core already waits 200 ms).
6. Experiment build: the detector model, its internal RAM and largest block, and the admission control.

No bench build was made and no unit was flashed. The queue file `~/stackchan-models/usb-queue.txt` was empty when checked; a
bench window is only worth asking for once S12 exists and passes the PC tests.

## 9. Decisions made here (so they can be overturned)

- Default setting is `talk`, not `always` (section 5).
- The idle watch of `always` runs at one frame a second as the brief said; two seconds in quiet mode and at night.
- The host's old search stays alive for units without the look tool, so the new "robot" default does not switch it off before
  the unit can replace it.
- Owner name of the hub on the unit: `home-hub`.
- A search and the turn it ends spend one of the four turns together; the return to center is free.
