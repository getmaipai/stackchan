# Fast eyes: why the face stutters and what was changed, 2026-11-16

Placement: unit firmware plus the settings page preview. Tree: `~/esp/stackchan-rebase/fast` (copy of the merge tree). Nothing is committed.
Patches: V20, V21, V22 (listed at the end). The merged image (sha256 `c5ccc3b6...56ac6c`) is the image on the unit; nothing from this work stays on it.

## Bottom line

The face is limited by LVGL's software drawing on the unit, not by the display link. A refresh spends 8 to 25 ms drawing and under 1.5 ms waiting for the SPI flush. The anime Big eyes (V10) cost about three times what the faces-core eyes (X02) cost per refresh. The changes here make the motion constants right (gaze snap, emotion settle) and cut the pixels per refresh by 20 to 40 percent, but they do not reach 30 refreshes a second while the eyes move or 25 while speaking. Getting there needs sprites for the eye body and the pupil and a cheaper mouth (below). That work is not done.

Update, same day: the sprite work below (V23 to V26) found that the screen refresh is bound by the SPI link to the panel, not by LVGL's drawing. The pictures are in, the faces look the same, and two buffers plus a smaller lock scope took the moving eyes from 27.8 to 32 to 33 refreshes a second on one image (see "Sprites, two buffers and the lock"). The slowest 1 percent of gaps is still 56 to 65 ms while the eyes move (goal: under 50). The first sections below are the V20 to V22 state and are kept as measured.

Per refresh, X02 drew in 4.2 ms idle and 6.0 ms moving; the anime eyes take 8 to 15 ms for the same work. That is the regression the owner sees: not fewer refreshes only, but each one three times as heavy, which also holds the screen lock longer (average wait of the face loop for the lock: 3.5 to 6.6 ms while moving or speaking, 0.7 to 1.1 ms idle). The p99 gap between refreshes stays 66 to 100 ms in every run, idle included.

Run to run noise is about 1 refresh per second. Not measured: p99 frame time under a real spoken turn (the bench workload feeds a synthetic level), the pickup, a 15 minute soak of the final image (see the end).

## Where the time goes

- Drawing: about 0.6 to 1.0 microseconds per flushed pixel. A Big eye is a rounded box, a pupil disc, two highlights and, with a lid, a quad drawn as four triangles; each is an anti-aliased LVGL draw call with a radius mask, over the screen color, over the panel.
- The mouth while speaking is 16 round-ended line segments of 8 px. Speaking runs at 10 to 12 ms per refresh on 10.6k pixels, mostly the mouth: the eyes do not change.
- The SPI flush is cheap (40 MHz, 0.2 to 1.4 ms per refresh).
- The face loop (20 ms timer, 2 to 3 ms per pass, 30 to 47 passes a second) and the LVGL task both run at priority 3 on core 1 and serialise on one lock. A 10 ms face loop made the drawing slower in wall time (14 ms to 24 ms idle) because it competes with the LVGL task, so that change was dropped. The 10 ms refresh timer and two 10-row buffers (the same 12.8 KB) were also tried; they gave no gain and were dropped too.
- Stock animations: the default skin's eyes and mouth do not use the toolkit animation durations any more (X01 and V10 replaced them); the only remaining ease curves are the ones in `maipai_facefx_core.h` and `maipai_look_core.h`, listed below.
- Anti-aliased arcs: not used; the eyes are rounded boxes and discs. The cost is the mask per draw call, not arcs.

## Stutter suspects (from the owner's list)

Measured or read, not proven as the cause of the stutter the owner sees:

- The listening ring task's update took up to 97 ms once in the merge note (budget 30 ms). Not reproduced in these runs; it needs a spoken turn with the listening ring on.
- Prefs or NVS reads on the face loop: TC02 made the PSRAM-stack tasks use cached values; the face task (internal stack) does not read NVS in these runs (its worst pass was under 10 ms at p99).
- Lock contention with the overlay timer: the face loop waits 3.5 to 6.6 ms for the lock while the screen is busy. The lock is held for the whole LVGL refresh, so a heavier refresh stalls the face loop directly.
- The audio decode raised above the screen task (Y03) was not isolated; speaking is 26 to 30 refreshes a second against 31 for X02.

## What changed

1. V20, counters (bench builds): per refresh drawing time against flush wait, lock wait and update time of the face loop, p50 and p99 of a refresh and of the gap, pixels per refresh, the face task's free stack, and `self.face.bench_mode dirty 0|1` to switch the old redraw back on so before and after come from one image. Plain images get two clock reads per pass.
2. V21, dirty rectangles: shapes of the eyes, the brows and the mouth are compared one by one; only shapes that changed invalidate, merged to at most two areas per eye (`hal/maipai_dirty_core.h`). The tint color is applied only when the 5-6-5 value changes, and over the shapes only (it was a whole-screen redraw on every step: 76.8k pixels per frame on the host model, 28k to 38k now). Checked on the host: the screen the unit holds equals a fresh full draw on every frame of a scripted run (765 frames, 0 bad pixels), and `host/tests/firmware/dirty_core_test.c` passes. A first version with thin strips along the outlines made the unit slower (more LVGL areas, 8 ms of CPU per pass) and overflowed the face task's 4 KB stack (the rectangle set was on the stack): it was removed; the scratch set is now one shared static of 384 bytes. The bench build reports 428 bytes of free stack on the face task with the final code (516 on the merged image).
3. V22, motion constants: the gaze is a damped spring (stiffness 2600, damping ratio 0.65), 90 percent of a jump in about 45 ms, settled in about 120 ms, 7 percent over, for both eyes and the mouth position; before it was an exponential of 70 ms that took 210 ms to reach 95 percent. The pose spring (emotion, lid, brows, mouth) is stiffness 900 at the same ratio 0.62, settled in about 210 ms (was 350 ms), with sub steps of 8 ms. The blink (140 ms: 55 ms shut, 85 ms open) and the mouth attack (tau 40 ms) already met the goal. `host/maipai_body/face_shapes.py` carries the same pose spring; the preview parity test passes against this tree.

Frame strips at 33 ms (host renderer, real LVGL): a gaze jump reaches about 70 percent in the first 33 ms and is at rest within 100 ms with a visible hint of overshoot; a blink is shut by 33 to 66 ms and open again by about 130 ms. Pictures: `fast/saccade.png`, `fast/strip.png` in the session scratch folder.

## Not done, and the plan that would reach the goal

The goal numbers need less drawing per refresh, not smarter rectangles:

- Eye body and pupil as RGB565A8 or A8 sprites in PSRAM, one per eye size (a blink steps through four sizes), moved by offset with an image object. A blit is a few cycles per pixel where the masked rounded box is about 150. The shape path stays for lids, cheeks and brows. Expected: the 8 to 15 ms eye cost drops to about 2 to 3 ms.
- The mouth as fewer segments (9 points instead of 17) or as one polygon: halves the 10 to 12 ms speaking cost. This changes the shared shape table and the preview parity data, so it goes spec first through `face_table.json`.
- Both are drawing-model changes; they need an owner look at the result before landing.

## Sprites, two buffers and the lock (V23 to V26)

Placement: unit firmware. Tree: `~/esp/stackchan-rebase/sprite` (the memory headroom tree, `mem`, plus V20 to V22). Nothing is committed.

### What the time really was

The first measurement blamed LVGL's software drawing (0.6 to 1.0 microseconds a flushed pixel). The sprite build says most of it is the link to the panel. LVGL's "render" time includes the wait inside the flush call, and the panel link (SPI, 40 MHz) takes about 0.5 microseconds a pixel: 28 thousand pixels while both Big eyes move is 56 KB, 12 to 14 ms. With one buffer, a refresh while the eyes moved took 15.7 ms of "render" as shape lists and 15.0 as pictures, the same, because the pixel count decides. The drawing method matters only where the old drawing was a masked draw: the cheek and lid triangles (an emotion change: 22.4 ms of render as shapes, 12.3 ms as pictures) and the sixteen round-ended lines of the speaking mouth (9.5 ms as shapes, 6.0 ms as a mask), measured on one image with one buffer.

Two things set the rate after that. LVGL had one 20 row buffer, so drawing and sending took turns (V26 makes it two 10 row buffers: the wait for the flush while the eyes move drops from 10.1 to 1.9 ms). And the refresh is a chain of serial work in two tasks of the same priority on one core: the update loop (the picture, the lock), then LVGL (drawing, the SPI), about 23 ms of work and 30 ms between refreshes while the eyes move. A 1000 Hz RTOS tick (CONFIG_FREERTOS_HZ=1000, tried as an image of its own) did not change it (29.6 refreshes a second, p99 gap 51 ms against 32.3 and 56.5 on the same build at 100 Hz), so it is not in the patches.

### What changed

- V23, pictures. `hal/maipai_sprite_core.h` (pure C, host tested in `host/tests/firmware/sprite_core_test.c`) rasterizes the shape lists of the approved design: one RGB565 plus alpha picture per eye (the rounded box with its corner edge, the pupil and the two highlights, the lid and cheek cut out of the alpha) and one alpha mask for the mouth (a round brush stamped along the 17 points at half-pixel places). LVGL blits each as one image. The picture is built when the screen is refreshed, not on every pass of the update loop. A heart, the closed-eye arc, an eye fading in or out and a stroke thicker than 36 stay shape draws. The dirty rectangles of V21 are untouched.
- V24, bench counters: slow gaps and what they were made of, lock hold per pass, picture cost, a FACERUN log line, and the A/B switches `sprite_off`, `ls_wide` and `one_buffer` on `self.face.bench_mode`.
- V25, lock scope: `StackChan::update()` is split. The servo and LED bus writes no longer happen with the screen lock held.
- V26, display buffers: two 10 row buffers instead of one 20 row buffer (the same 12.8 KB of internal DMA memory).

### Look: the same faces

`scripts/fw-face-render` draws the real LVGL and the real face classes; `sprite off` draws shape lists, `sprite on` the pictures. ### Numbers on the unit (one bench image, refreshes a second, 8 to 10 s per run)

Image `spr-bench-10`: pictures on or off, the lock scope and the two buffers switched at run time with `self.face.bench_mode`, the faces drawn one after the other so the unit, the temperature and the clock are the same. "Shapes" is the drawing of V22 (shape lists), "Pictures" is V23 with V25 and V26.

| Workload | Shapes, one buffer (V22 state, bench-8) | Shapes, two buffers | Pictures, two buffers, lock scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eyes move (Big eyes jump every 50 ms) | 27.8 | 31.5 and 31.2 | 32.9 (soak median 32.3, 6 runs) |
| Speaking (synthetic level) | 33.4 | 35.1 and 34.5 | 31.0 (soak median 31.5) |
| Emotion changes | 16.5 | 16.6 and 16.9 | 17.4 (soak median 19.5) |
| p99 gap, eyes move (ms) | 61 | 58 and 65 | 61 (soak median 56.5) |
| p99 gap, speaking (ms) | 97 | 97 and 100 | 92 (soak median 95.5) |
| Render per refresh, eyes move (ms) | 15.7 | 16.3 | 14.1 |
| Wait for the flush, eyes move (ms) | 10.1 | 1.4 | 2.4 |
| Face loop lock hold per pass, eyes move (ms) | 2.7 | 2.5 | 6.0 (building two pictures, 2.1 ms each) |

Where this leaves the goal: at least 30 refreshes a second while the eyes move (32.3 to 32.9, met), at least 28 speaking (31.0 to 31.5, met), p99 gap under 50 ms (56.5 to 61 while the eyes move, not met; speaking 92 to 95 is the synthetic speech's own silences, a still face draws nothing and a gap of 100 ms or more is counted in the last bin). The pictures are a small win for emotion changes and for the eyes moving (3 to 5 percent) and a loss for the speaking face (10 percent: the mouth mask is rebuilt every frame and its brush every time the thickness changes). Pictures cost 2.1 ms per eye to build: that is PSRAM write and read traffic (30 KB an eye) and it is serial work on the same core. They are on by default; `bench_mode sprite_off 1` turns them off on a bench image.

What would move the p99 gap: fewer pixels while the eyes jump (the link is the limit) or a faster link (80 MHz for the SPI clock, not tried: the panel's data sheet limit is lower than that). A picture cache that rewrites only the pupil when the body is unchanged would halve the 2.1 ms. Neither is done.

The unit's own frames (the screen read back over the cable) for both drawings, Big eyes happy and a steady speaking mouth: `~/esp/stackchan-rebase/sprite/logs/w9a-old_two_buffers-*.png`, `w9a-sprites_lock_two_buffers-*.png`, with `unit_cmp_happy.png` and `unit_cmp_speak60.png` side by side. The mouth is pixel for pixel the same on the unit; the eyes differ only where they look (the gaze moved between the two captures).

### Unit windows (this work)

All with the USB lock, the app partition only, read back, ended on the known-good merged image (sha256 `c5ccc3b6...56ac6c`, read back each time). Bench images: three minutes with zero panic before any measurement.

- `spr-bench-8` (pictures, lock scope, two buffers): 23 workloads in 180 s, 0 panic, then the A/B above.
- `spr-bench-10` (the final code): 23 workloads in 180 s, 0 panic, then the A/B.
- `spr-hz-10` (the same, tick at 1000 Hz): 20 workloads in 170 s, 0 panic; no gain, not shipped.
- Found and fixed on the way: the new bench counters overflowed the main task's 6 KB stack (the four histograms of a snapshot are now static); the serial reply of `bench_run` can be cut by another task's log line (the answer is also one FACERUN log line).
- Found, not fixed (not in these patches): twice in nine boots a bench image stopped at boot with `assert failed: xQueueGenericSendFromISR queue.c:1180 (pxQueue)`. Decoded with the build's ELF: `dvp_vsync_isr` (esp_cam_ctlr_dvp_cam.c:429) running during `esp_cam_new_dvp_ctlr_ext` (line 891), which enables the V-SYNC interrupt in `gpio_isr_handler_add` before the controller's event queue exists. It is a race in the vendor camera driver at camera start, not in the face code; the images had M06 (camera frames in PSRAM) and the race may be more likely with it. A plain boot after the reset was fine both times.

## Unit windows (V20 to V22, earlier)

Three short windows with the USB lock, each ended on the merged image (read back, sha256 `c5ccc3b6...56ac6c`). Window 1 (first bench build) reset six times with a stack overflow in the face task, decoded with the build's ELF at `vApplicationStackOverflowHook`; that is fixed (see V21). Windows 2 and 3 ran the corrected build for about 10 minutes in total with no reset, assert or task watchdog. That is not the 15 minute soak the rules ask for; the image is not left on the unit.
