## The new games

- **Tic-Tac-Toe** (`tictactoe`). The 3 by 3 board fills the whole play area (owner request, later the same day): x 20 to 300, y 28 to 184, nine cells of 93 by 52 px (the right column 94) with 2 px lines between them; the touch regions tile the area exactly, so a tap anywhere in a cell counts. Cell rects (x, y, w, h): row 1 at y 28: (20,28,93,52) (113,28,93,52) (206,28,94,52); row 2 at y 80 and row 3 at y 132 the same. Marks are large (X lines 34 px across, 7 px thick; O ring 40 px across, 7 px thick). The three rows are the level buttons at the start: Easy (random), Medium (takes a win, blocks yours), Hard (perfect play by negamax with alpha-beta, random among equally good cells). The person is X and goes first. The status line ("Your turn", "I played row R column C", the result) sits under the board at y 200 and 218, text only, no tap target. Score: 10, 20 or 30 for a win by level, 3, 6 or 9 for a draw, nothing for a loss.
- **Do It** (`bopit`, reworked by `UI14`). Six large tiles in a 3 by 2 grid under a call-out box: Tap, Swipe left, Swipe right, Shake, Pet head, Tilt (each 92 by 49 px, y 82 to 182, inside the UI08 safe area; the call-out box y 28 to 78 is text only and not a button). The call-out names the move and the matching tile lights up. Pressing the matching tile or doing the physical move (swipe left or right, shake, pet the head, tilt any way) counts; the wrong tile or the wrong move ends the run. The time to answer starts at 3.2 s and loses a twelfth each round (never under 650 ms); a late move ends the run. Takes its taps on release so a swipe is not also a tap. A cue tone per call starts with the call.
- **Rock Paper Scissors** (`rps`). Three big tiles across the whole play area, each 92 px wide and 102 px tall: rock (20,80,92,102), paper (114,80,92,102), scissors (208,80,92,102), with large drawn icons and a label. The unit's pick is drawn at the top with a sound for the result; the result line is under the tiles. A tie plays again; the score is the win streak, the first loss ends the run.

All tap targets lie inside y 28 to 184 (the UI08 safe area), are at least 44 px high, and never touch each other; a test checks every pack.

## How a sound starts with its picture

The Color Memory fix (UI03) holds a tone until the first display strip that carries its tile. The same rule now covers every game: a rule names the region its picture changes in (the `sync` action) and the sound waits for the first strip that overlaps that region, or 100 ms on a dark screen. A rule with no `sync` plays at once. Whack-a-mole syncs a hit and an escape to the mole's cell and a miss to the tapped cell, Tic-Tac-Toe a mark to its cell, Do It a cue to the cue box, Rock Paper Scissors a result to the tapped button.

## Inputs from the unit's own sensors

The imu task and the head touch task run on PSRAM stacks, so they never touch flash or NVS (`scripts/psram-stack-lint.py` passes, 0 unreviewed paths). They call three functions (`maipai_games_post_input`, `maipai_games_imu_feed`, `maipai_games_shake`) that use atomics only: no lock, no allocation, no flash. The game's 30 ms tick reads them.

- Shake: the existing motion detector (threshold 9 m/s^2 over 100 ms) posts one event.
- Pet: a press on the head strip posts one event.
- Tilt: a new detector reads the accelerometer every 100 ms. The pose the unit rests in when a game starts is its level; a tilt is the pull leaving it by about 22 degrees on one axis for two samples, and it must come back within 0.15 g before the next one counts. Nothing counts while the pull's length is not one g (the unit is moving) or right after a shake. Directions follow the host's orientation table (the unit's right ear down reads +x, face down reads -z).
- Swipes: a finger moving left or right inside the play area, judged by the same rule as the page swipes (36 px, 900 ms, 60 px/s). Swipes that start in the side edges stay the volume's.

## UI15 and the final merged image (2026-11-17)

`UI15` adds two data-pack games, Magic 8-Ball (`eightball`) and Dice and Coin (`dice`), on small engine additions: a die shape and a coin shape, a ring-light action, a celebrate action, four more sound clips (rattle, thud, mystic hum, coin ring), 32 texts and 64 shapes per pack. Voice: "play magic 8 ball", "ask the magic 8 ball", "roll a dice", "flip a coin", "play dice" and Spanish forms start the game by the local command path (the host answers before the model); "flip a coin" starts Dice and Coin, where the Coin tile is one tap away.

- **Gentle shake.** The games get every 20 ms accelerometer sample and use their own detector: strokes of 2.2 m/s^2 (0.22 g) on the moving axis, three within 0.9 s, 0.8 s quiet time afterwards. A wobble of 3 cm at 3 Hz (about 10 m/s^2), 1.5 cm at 3 Hz (about 5) and 1 cm at 2.5 Hz (about 3) all count, and a 5 m/s^2 wobble counts in under half a second. A tiny tremor (1 m/s^2), a lone bump, a slow roll and standing still do not. At 20 m/s^2 (2 g) or more the shake is too hard: it posts `MG_EV_EASY`, the game answers "Easy!" and gives no points. Hints and tiles say "Gentle shake" / "Agita suave" (core test `test_gentle_shake`).
- **Celebrations.** A won Tic-Tac-Toe game, a won Rock Paper Scissors round and every new best score (place 0 of the saved list, never a loss) start the overlay engine: confetti for a win, confetti and fireworks for 4 s for a new best, with the happy face, through `maipai_fx_celebrate` (the governor sheds it, the banner row and subtitle strip stay clear, and it does nothing when overlays are off). The overlay engine paints over the finished frame, so a game cannot put it behind its text; the confetti is sparse and the result lines stay readable (unit snapshot `19_ttt_win_confetti.png`).
- **Motion.** Arithmetic on existing objects, no heap: the mole pops with an ease-out overshoot and is squashed when struck, with stars circling; the dice hop and bounce in turn, the coin flips edge-on, bounces and glints; the eight ball wobbles on a damped spring and its window fades in and floats; the Rock Paper Scissors hands bump with the beat and the unit's pick fades in; Tic-Tac-Toe marks draw themselves in; Do It's called tile pulses. Not done: dice rotation (LVGL objects do not rotate cheaply), the Tic-Tac-Toe winning-line sweep, the Do It timer ring and score count-up.
- **Frame gaps** (bench build, `self.game.bench_gaps`, gaps between display strips while a game moves, no tool call during the window): Magic 8-Ball p50 5 ms, p99 45 to 70 ms over three runs (worst 160 to 255); Dice p50 5 ms, p99 55 to 140 ms (worst 200 to 255). The goal of p99 under 50 ms was met once (8-ball, 45 ms) and not reliably. A game standing still showed p99 up to 255 ms in the same measurements, so some of the stalls are the rest of the firmware (the face underneath, Wi-Fi and the roaming code), not the motion; the dice amplitudes were reduced and the clips are rendered at the game's start, which did not change the numbers.
- **Memory and size.** Final plain image 3,554,928 bytes (final7 plain 3,537,504; the rest of the difference is the RM roaming patches). Pack sizes: whack 1,7xx, rps 3,380 (the hands, the face and the countdown), eightball 3,6xx, dice 2,3xx bytes of flash data; at runtime the Rock Paper Scissors screen holds about 60 shapes (about 10 KB of internal RAM while it runs). During the bench games internal_free stayed above 59 KB (lowest 59,779).
